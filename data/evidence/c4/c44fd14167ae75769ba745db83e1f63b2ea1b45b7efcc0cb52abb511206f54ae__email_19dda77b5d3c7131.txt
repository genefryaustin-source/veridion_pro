What’s up
Dogecoin Having A Day
Dogecoin (DOGE) just hit a dime. It’s up 7% in the past 24 hours. At one point earlier today, DOGE actually crossed 11 cents. That hasn’t happened since February.
“Traders continue to pour capital into DOGE futures,” U.Today noted.
Just in the past day, open Interest (OI) shot up 28% to $1.8B, the highest level for DOGE futures activity since October. The upward jaunt caught DOGE shorts off guard, “creating a squeeze that lifted the price higher,” U.Today ( https://u.today/meme-coins-rally-doge-hits-01-in-short-squeeze-shib-jumps-on-golden-cross )added.

What's down
Bitcoin's Quest For $80K Pauses At Least Until After Fed Chair's Swan Song
Bitcoin is mired in a mud flat just below $77K. The largest crypto has shed 2% since last week at this time. 
Rising oil prices continue to weigh on risk assets. Brent crude futures topped $115 a barrel. That's now seven trading sessions in a row in which the international crude oil benchmark has gone higher.
The Fed also wraps up its meeting later today so for traders there’s a kind of a deer-in-headlights dynamic at play. No one is expecting a rate cut. But this will be Jerome Powell’s last meeting as Chair. 
What’s he going to say?
“The impact of the ongoing conflict in the Middle East will be the focus of Powell’s remarks,” Deutsche Bank economists said. “With uncertainty still pervasive, we expect he will emphasize that officials are unsure of the precise fallout from the war on the economy and monetary policy.”
President Donald Trump’s nominee to replace Powell, Kevin Warsh, is expected to be to confirmed by the Senate. Meanwhile, the Justice Department said it would close a criminal investigation into Powell in connection with an overly costly central bank headquarters refurbishment project.
The Senate Banking Committee is expected to vote today to advance Warsh's nomination to the full chamber for final confirmation (CNBC).

What's next
DeFi 'Bent But Not Broken'
The $292 million KelpDAO exploit no doubt exposed systemic risk across lending protocols. But the DeFi realm remains viable and on the right track.
That’s the view of Geoffrey Kendrick, a digital asset researcher at Standard Chartered.
In a note titled, “DeFi-Bent Not Broken,” Kendrick argues that the response to the incident by a coalition of industry players led by Aave could well prove to be a turning point for the better. 
DeFi’s biggest risk is complexity, Kendrick said. The Kelp episode, he explained, exposed an asset-liability mismatch inside lending markets as increasingly complex collateral — including wrapped, staked, and restaked assets — sat against liabilities with very different risk characteristics. The mismatch was amplified by concentrated looping trades, the kind of “leverage-maximizing” structure DeFi is designed to enable when things work normally. “It’s also the kind of structure that can magnify contagion” when things do not work normally, The Block said.
Standard Chartered still expects growth in DeFi and, relatedly, is predicting that tokenized real-world assets (RWA) will reach $2 trillion ( https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SwYN7mTi6HM&list=RDSwYN7mTi6HM&start_radio=1 ) by the end of 2028. That RWA total stands at about $40B today.
